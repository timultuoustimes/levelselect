You have been writing things down in this app since the first build. A note on a session, what you thought when you finished something, why you put a game down. All of it was real, dated, synced — and every word of it was only ever visible inside the sheet you typed it into.

So the Stats tab is the **Journal** now, and the charts are one of the things inside it.

### Everything you've written, in order [new]

The timeline is built from what was already there. Nothing new had to be stored to show it.

**A day of a game is one entry.** Three sessions of Hollow Knight on a Saturday used to be three rows saying the same game's name three times; now it's one — *"5h 59m · 2 sessions"* — and tapping it gives you the day in full: each session with its own note and time, any runs, and whether that was the evening you finished it.

Tap to read, press and hold to edit. Reading is what a journal is for, and tapping used to open an editor.

**And it asks.** When you stop a session, it asks what happened — once, while it's still what you were just doing. That's the note that makes a journal worth re-reading, and before this the only way to write one was to go back and edit a session you'd already finished.

Home is a scroll and the Journal is a closed book, which is the actual difference between them: one is the log you're still working through, the other is the history already written.

### Things that happened before you had this [new]

The app could only hold what it watched. Your library starts the day you installed it, and the part of your gaming life worth writing down mostly doesn't.

**Memories** fix that. Something that happened, when it happened, who you were with, where — and pictures.

The date input is the whole point. Every other date field insists you know the day, and the entries people most want to write are the ones they know least precisely. So how well you know it is a *choice*: an exact day, a month, a year, or **not sure at all**.

*"Christmas 1995 or 1996"* is a real answer. Write it that way and that's how it's shown — always your words, never re-rendered from a date the app made up. If you know the day but not the year, say so, and it sits on Christmas Day of the first year with a mark saying the year is uncertain. It won't quietly file it under 1 January, which is nearer the *previous* Christmas than either one you meant.

Pictures go on a memory. One becomes the header, several become a grid, and any of them opens full size.

### A year of play, in one screen [new]

The Journal has three views now: **Timeline**, **Calendar**, **Charts**.

The calendar opens on a year, because thirty years of history one month at a time is three hundred and sixty screens of scrolling. A year is twelve small months and a heat map — where you played, how much, all at once. Scrub the years along the top; ones with something in them stand out, and empty ones are still tappable, because the year you have nothing in is exactly the year you're about to write your first console into.

Tap a month and it opens properly: full box art on every day you played, the memory's own photo where there is one, and what the month added up to underneath.

Any empty day is a **+**. Tapping the 14th is the same as adding a memory and then setting its date, minus the setting.

### Light mode, in your colour [new]

The whole app can be light now — System, Light or Dark, in Settings.

It isn't the dark theme with the colours swapped. Every surface knows which way "up" is: a card lifts off a dark ground by getting lighter and off a light one by getting darker, so the same screen reads correctly either way.

**And the background is yours.** Accent and background sit on one screen, so you can see a button against the ground you're choosing while you choose it. The app's own four colours come first, then a palette, then a hue wheel if you want to go looking. Pick a background and the app keeps its own contrast — your colour, its readability — so there's no way to choose one that makes text disappear.

The *Continue Playing* card follows the ground it sits on, and the widgets follow your accent into light mode too.

### Ten statuses that say what they mean [improved]

**Old Favorite** is new, and it's for the games that don't fit the others: the ones you've played to death, that you'll play again, whether or not you ever technically finished them. Endlessly replayable isn't the same as unfinished, and it isn't the same as done.

**Every status now says what it means**, in a line, right where you pick it. Nine shipped without that, and the cost showed up in the obvious way — statuses nobody could remember the point of.

**And you can rename any of them.** If *Shelved* is *On the shelf* to you, make it that.

### Smaller things [improved]

- **Your backup includes your memories.** It didn't. The words and the photographs were both missing from the export, quietly — which is the worst way for a backup to be wrong.
- **A picture can be taken off a memory again**, which it couldn't.
- **Deleting a memory takes its photos with it** instead of leaving them occupying space you can't see.
- **Text boxes grow.** Four of them stopped at a fixed height, which turned the field into a tiny scroller at exactly the moment you had something to say.
- **A game you're waiting for gets re-checked.** Release dates were fetched once and never asked about again, so a delay could never reach you. Games that haven't come out yet are looked up daily now, and their reminders move with them.
- **Fixed:** a memory with no game attached looked greyed out, as though it weren't really there.
