### RetroAchievements, visibly

- **Every imported achievement wears its badge.** The art RA's community drew for each achievement now sits on its row — full colour once you've earned it, RA's own greyed variant while you haven't, so nothing unearned ever looks earned. One switch in Appearance turns the art off if you prefer plain rows. Already-installed sets pick their badges up on the next refresh.
- **A trophy shelf in Stats.** Every set you've mastered or completed, as a wall of RA's game icons — gold border for hardcore mastery, because that distinction is RA's and the app keeps it honest. Tap any icon to open that set on RetroAchievements.
- **Doors out.** The set's page on RA from the tracker and from the game's link row, and your own profile from RetroAchievements settings — all in the in-app browser.

### Rolling credits is a moment, not a checklist

- **Mark as Beaten.** From a game's page (the Beaten section, or the ⋯ menu): beat the game, 100%, New Game+, or your own words. Beating a game and finishing every item on your tracker are different achievements — now both are recordable and neither pretends to be the other. "100%" means all of *your* list, the one you chose to keep; it doesn't claim a platinum.
- **Dates as fuzzy as memory.** An exact day for last night's credits, a month for "sometime that spring," a bare year for the game you beat in 2011 — stored and shown only as precisely as you actually know. Logging your history needs no sessions and no retroactive box-ticking.
- **Stats answers "what did I beat this year?"** The Completions card becomes Beaten & Completed: each year shows both counts and opens into the actual list of games.
- **Status shelves grow up.** Every shelf (Completed, Backlog, all of them) now sorts — name, last played, recently added, rating, release year — and can show as a cover grid.
