The tempting fixes were all structural — tighter shelf geometry, density tiers, a hierarchy that told you which row mattered most. None of them would have worked, because the page was not badly arranged. It had nothing at its center.

Everything below follows from putting you there.

### Three places, three jobs

- **Home is you.** Your picture and name at the top, and behind them the covers of everything you played in the last seven days — so the page looks different depending on how the week went. A quiet week falls back to one game's artwork rather than going blank. Three numbers underneath, chosen because all three move: playing, this week, total.
- **Library is the collection.** Systems, collections, and every status that isn't what you're doing right now.
- **Stats is your history.** How much you've played, what you finished, which systems and genres you actually reach for.
- **Wishlist is a tab, and only a tab.** It was also a shelf inside Library, which made no sense when it has its own place at the bottom of the screen.
- **"See all" lands in Library, filtered** to the shelf you came from, instead of dropping you at the top of everything.

### A profile

- **Settings → Your profile.** A picture, a name, and your handles. It draws nothing until you put something in it — a placeholder ring above "Add your name" is a form, and a form is the opposite of a personal page.
- **The picture can come from anywhere you already have one.** Photos, any game's artwork in your library, or a sticker — Memoji, Genmoji, or one you cut out of your own photo. The app decides what to do with it by looking at the image: something with a transparent background is already the subject, so it goes straight in; a photo or a wide painting asks you to choose which square survives.
- **Handles ask in the order the thing actually happens.** Add a handle once, then tick everywhere you use it — rather than eight fields where seven get the same word typed into them. Sign up somewhere new later and you add the service to the handle you already have.
- **Services you name yourself.** Apple Arcade, a storefront, a forum. The list of places people play isn't something this app can finish guessing.
- **Handles are decoration and the app says so.** Nothing signs in, nothing is shared, nothing is fetched. Your RetroAchievements username is the one handle that *does* something, and it stays where it was: in the Keychain, on your device, never synced.

### Four game page layouts

- **Showcase, Classic, Cover and Compact.** Build 32 rebuilt the game page around one shape; this gives you the shape back. Classic is close to the original, Cover leads with box art, Compact is nearly all list.
- Each one has its own art mask and its own point where the title hands over to the navigation bar, rather than one set of numbers stretched across four very different compositions.

### Logos find themselves

- **Game pages fetch a wordmark automatically** instead of waiting for you to choose one per game — which, across a few hundred games, meant almost nobody ever saw the feature build 32 shipped. IGDB first, SteamGridDB after, the game's name as text when neither has one.
- **Settings → Use game logos** turns it off if you'd rather always read the name.

### Stats cards turn over

- **Tap the chart icon in a card's corner** to flip between bars and a pie. Library, Ratings, By System, Genres and Tags all flip; your choice sticks per card.
- **Tap a slice, or its legend row,** and the middle of the ring tells you that slice's count and share — the question a pie usually raises and refuses to answer.
- The hole is a real hole, so the total can never be covered by the chart drawn around it.

### Colors you can actually choose

- **A color editor of our own**, replacing the system picker: type a hex, keep a color with the +, cancel if you change your mind, and put one color back to its default without resetting the rest.
- **Your name on Home can take a color** — including "Accent", which keeps following your accent as you change it rather than freezing a copy of today's.

### Smaller things [improved]

- **Play is filled and in your accent.** It was the most important control on Home and the lightest thing on the screen.
- **Green means one thing now: a timer is running.** It used to be both the Play button's color and the running-timer color, which are opposites.
- **The Continue Playing card says where you left off** before it says how long you've played.
- Console names use their short forms on Home too — *SNES*, not the full name.
- The Mac settings screen is reachable and readable, and the Mac toolbar is glass across all four tabs.
- Fixed: Home broke at the largest accessibility text sizes.
- Fixed: a hard line sat under the toolbar where the header art met it.

### Known [fixed]

On macOS the tab selector at the top doesn't take your accent color. That's macOS declining it rather than the app failing to ask — it looks fixable when macOS 27 ships.
