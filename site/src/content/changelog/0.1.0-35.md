Stats got the treatment Home, Library and Wishlist have had: it says what it is now. And the wishlist learned to count — a countdown on the card, a widget, a calendar, and a reminder the morning before, all pointed at the day the game arrives **on the platform you chose**.

That last part turned out to be harder than it sounds, and finding out why fixed something that had been wrong for a long time.

### Stats is your history, in three questions

Fourteen cards in one scroll read as fourteen charts. They're grouped now by the question each answers:

- **Time** — the overview, recent play, by month, your streak, most played
- **Finishes** — what you've completed, and your RetroAchievements masteries
- **Library** — ratings, status, system, genres, series, tags, release year

The group is fixed; the order *inside* a group is still yours, so an arrangement you made before this update survives exactly as it was.

**Overview moved above all three.** It counts games, hours, sessions and percent-finished — it's the total the groups break down, so it isn't one of them. It's tiles with icons now rather than four numbers in a row, and it stays at the top.

**Latest Finishes** is new: the last five games you finished, with covers. Unlike Home's recently-beaten shelf it has no time limit, because a shelf that empties is right for a prompt and wrong for the page about your history.

**The streak remembers half a year.** Fifteen unlabelled weeks became twenty-six with the months named — you could see a gap before but not when it was, and a streak worth being proud of scrolled out of view in under four months.

### A game you're waiting for tells you when [new]

Four things, and they all count to the same day:

- **The wishlist card counts down.** "in 3 days" while it's near, the date itself once it's further off. The switch is a fortnight, because that's exactly where the countdown stops speaking in days — "in 2 days" is a fact about your week, and "in 6 weeks" tells you less than "Oct 16" does.
- **Two widgets.** *Coming Soon* is covers with the wait under each; *Release Calendar* is the dates themselves, and at the large size it draws a real month with covers standing where the day numbers would be.
- **A release calendar in the app**, month by month, including the games with no announced date — a calendar that quietly drops half your wishlist is lying about what you're waiting for.
- **A reminder at 9am**, on the day or up to a week before. A release is a calendar date, so firing *at* it would wake you in the small hours for a game that isn't out where you are yet.

Reminders can break through silent mode and Focus, and so can the still-playing reminder — one switch, on by default, in Settings.

### Which date is yours [new]

A game can be out on PC and months away on Switch 2. The app could only ever hold one date, so it had to pick, and it picked using whichever platform IGDB listed first — a platform you may never have chosen.

Now every platform's date is kept, and **the one you see is the one for the platform you picked**. Change it and the date follows immediately. The game page lists what a game is still coming to, each with its own date and countdown.

If your platform has no announced day, it says so rather than borrowing a date from somebody else's copy.

### Adding a game shows you the game [new]

The confirm screen was a cover, a name and a year — enough to tell two sequels apart and not enough for anything else. It now has the trailer, screenshots, the description, and the developer, publisher and genre.

It also shows **the release date the platform picker was always quietly deciding**, updating as you change platform, and the platform you choose is remembered rather than used once and thrown away.

Covers, screenshots and trailers all open full size, and trailers play in the app. Deku Deals and IGDB open in the app too, so checking a price doesn't lose the search you were in the middle of.

### Dates that had been wrong for months [fixed]

Onimusha: Way of the Sword sat under "No date yet" while IGDB had 4 September the whole time. Chasing it turned up the cause, and it wasn't small:

**IGDB moved where release-date precision lives** — from `category` to `date_format` — and the app was still asking for the old field. Every date came back looking imprecise, so exact days were discarded and replaced with "sometime that year". *Every game added since IGDB made that change* had been getting this.

The app asks for every release-date field now rather than naming them, so the next rename can't be silent.

Three smaller date bugs came out with it, all the same mistake — a calendar date read in local time:

- A year-only placeholder is stored at UTC midnight, and west of Greenwich that reads as the *previous* year. The field that exists to be re-asked was therefore never re-asked.
- Game Info showed the year in local time, so a 2026 placeholder displayed as 2025.
- The placeholder itself was written in local time, which hid the first bug from itself.

**And a beta is not a release.** Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 showed as released on 28 August — that's the beta. IGDB gives one row per release *event*, and the app took the first one. It takes the launch now: 16 October on Switch 2, 23 October elsewhere.

### Smaller things [improved]

- **The default accent is torch orange.** The app's background is dark purple and the default accent was purple, so every tinted control sat too close to what was behind it. If you've chosen your own accent, nothing changes.
- **Widgets follow your accent** for the first time, including the Live Activity.
- **The Now Playing widget is gone** — an "Open To…" launcher pointed at Now Playing is the same shelf from a widget that also does collections and systems, at three sizes instead of two.
- **Settings answers "is my library safe" first.** Sync moved to the top, and it's a shorter screen.
- **itch.io connects.** Games you've bought or claimed there come in, with their art. It's read-only, your token stays on the device, and it never passes through our server.
- **Hand-added games get covers.** Anything IGDB has never heard of now gets its artwork from SteamGridDB by name, instead of staying a grey rectangle.
- **The game page has trailers** and larger screenshots.
- **Fixed:** the logo picker let you choose a logo while logos were switched off, and then didn't draw it. It says so now.
