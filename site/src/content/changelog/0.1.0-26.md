### Generation

- **The generator knows which game it is.** Every request now carries the game's database identity — and its genres, themes and a one-line summary — instead of a bare name. A 2026 Pokémon sandbox no longer gets a mainline Pokémon plan because the model recognized the franchise; spin-offs, remakes and games that share a title with something older all plan as themselves.
- **Counted categories actually generate.** A set the plan called "one counter up to 700" used to fail after ninety seconds with advice to rename the category — the count was never sent to the server. It is now, and a counter comes back as a counter.
- **A failure names the real problem.** The wrong-game error no longer blames a category name the plan itself chose.

### Runs

- **A run's loadout comes from your tracker.** The keepsake picker is your Keepsakes category — filtered to the ones you've unlocked — and aspects narrow to the weapon you chose. No more typing "Old Spiked Collar" by hand.
- **Ending a run asks what only the end knows** — which gods showed up, where you fell.
- **Analytics, once you have a few runs.** Win rate by weapon, by keepsake, by anything picked from a list — with the raw fraction always beside the percentage, because "100%" alone and "1/1" say very different things. Free-typed fields never aggregate; typos would fragment every group.
- **History can filter to wins or losses** once there's enough of it.

### Stats

- **The depth the screen was missing:** your finished share, average rating *with how many games it's an average of*, the star distribution, hours and finishes by month, and a fifteen-week play heatmap.
- **Every slice is a door.** Genres, tags, series and release years don't just show counts — tapping one opens everything in your library that matches, the same screens the game page links to.
- **By system**, counted the way the library groups them.

### Widgets

- **Cover art survives Clear and Tinted Home Screens.** Covers now stay full-color instead of flattening into white rectangles.

### First run

- **A welcome, once.** New installs get one screen — what the app is, and that there's nothing to sign up for — then the same two buttons the empty shelf offers. No tour, and it never comes back.

### Search

- **Search reaches everything a game page shows.** Developer, publisher, genre, theme, perspective, mode and platform — typing "Capcom" surfaces everything Capcom made or published, without needing a filter for it.

### Wishlist

- **A wishlist of your own**, beside the Deku Deals view instead of behind it. Games you're watching live in the app now; Deku stays one tap away for prices.
- **The button says "Add to Wishlist"**, because that's what it does.
- **iPad no longer squeezes the wishlist into a 440-point strip.**

### Honesty

- **Achievement lists say where they came from.** An imported RetroAchievements set is labeled as the real, authored list; a generated set is labeled as a draft. The two never dress alike.
- **The privacy policy now covers RetroAchievements** — including the part worth knowing: your API key travels from your device to RetroAchievements directly, and never through this app's servers.
- **Each system appears once.** The library's system filter and headings no longer offer two identical "Switch" rows that quietly filtered different games.
- **The CSV importer asks which app your spreadsheet came from** — only when there's one to name, and it's the most useful answer on the form.
