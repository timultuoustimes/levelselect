A library is full of words somebody else chose. The console is a Mega Drive or a Genesis depending on where you grew up. A game is *owned* or it isn't, except you borrowed it from a friend for a couple of weeks in 1997 and beat it anyway.

This build is mostly about giving those words back.

### Consoles go by the name you call them [new]

**Settings → System names.** Mega Drive or Genesis. Super Nintendo or Super Famicom. TurboGrafx-16 or PC Engine. Pick one and it changes every shelf, chip and widget that names that system.

The ones you own are listed first, because those are the three you'll actually change. The rest are still there with a note saying your choice is remembered for when you do.

Renaming changes the label and nothing else. Your games keep the platform they were stored with, so a rename can never split one shelf into two.

### How you have a game, in your words [new]

The ownership row used to offer three answers. It now offers nine, and you choose which of them your library uses:

**Physical · Digital · Emulated · Former** — the ones you have.
**Subscription · Rented · Borrowed · Shared · Arcade** — the ones you only reach.

Four of those are new. **Rented** is the weekend from a shop that no longer exists. **Borrowed** is a friend's cartridge or a library loan. **Shared** is a household — family sharing, a home console. **Arcade** is the one where the copy never comes to you: you go to it, pay per play, and leave it there. You can beat a game you only ever played in an arcade, and now you can say so.

All four are off by default, so a row nobody configures is still five chips. Turning one off only hides it — a game already marked with it keeps the mark and keeps showing it, so nothing you recorded is ever lost to a vocabulary choice.

And you can order them: the app's own order, most-used-in-your-library first, or drag them where you want.

### One place to choose a color [new]

Accent and ground in a single editor, because they're chosen against each other. Light and dark are previewed **side by side** with the contrast ratio on each, so you can see what a color does to the appearance you're not currently in.

The swatches only offer colors that stay readable. Pick a hue and a saturation and the app supplies the brightness for each appearance — which means no color you can choose can make text unreadable, and where it has to soften a saturation to get there, it says so rather than quietly doing it.

### Browse by genre [new]

Every game has carried genres and themes since it was imported, and until now you could only reach them from a game that already had one. Library lists them now, largest first, counted from the games you're actually looking at.

### The tag field suggests words [new]

Forty-six subgenre terms — Metroidvania, Soulslike, Immersive Sim, Boomer Shooter, Cozy — offered under the tag field. Curated around where the metadata stops answering the question: calling Hollow Knight, Celeste and Super Meat Boy all "Platform / Adventure / Indie" is true and no help.

**Nothing is applied for you.** Tap one and it becomes an ordinary tag, indistinguishable from one you typed. Free text still works exactly as it did.

### Who made it [new]

Game Info now shows the director, designer, writer and composer where they're known. The studio comes from IGDB, which has no person-level credits at all; the names come from Wikidata, and the section says which is which. Where the two disagree about a release year, you're told — not corrected.

### Seasons, decades, and dates you're not sure about [new]

A memory can be filed to a season or a decade, not just a day, a month or a year. And "Christmas 1995 or 1996" is a thing you can now write down: it lands on both candidate Christmases and says it's uncertain, rather than being flattened to 1 January.

### Time played before you started tracking [new]

Steam says 42 hours. A Switch profile says 9. Those hours are real and there's no session history behind them, so a game page now takes a **starting total** — a number that adds to your time and stays out of your session history, because it never happened on any one day.

### Everything now happens in the app [new]

**Send feedback** goes straight to Tim from inside the app, with an "include diagnostics" switch and a button to read every word before it sends. **What's New** and **What's Coming** read this site rather than opening it in a browser.

Nothing is ever sent until you tap Send. Titles, notes, your profile and any keys are never included, and the screen says so.

### Recently Deleted holds pictures too [new]

Deleting a picture puts it in Recently Deleted with everything else, restorable for 30 days, with Delete Forever when you're sure. The 30-day promise is now on the screen where the clock is actually running.

### Settings is an index [improved]

Sixteen rows instead of nine long screens. Each one says what it holds.

### Also improved

- One shelf header everywhere, and the tab titles sit at full size on the toolbar row.
- The game page's status and platform stack rather than truncating when they don't fit side by side.
- Home says what its shelves are for while your library is new, and stops once it isn't.
- Ownership, status and star words all sync, so a phrase you chose is a phrase your iPad uses.
- Status colors reach the Home Screen, the Lock Screen and the watch.
- Press and hold any cover for Deku Deals and IGDB.

### Fixed

- A cover you picked from your own photos was invisible on every shelf, the Home header, the Journal and Recently Deleted. It draws everywhere now.
- Two "single" records meeting after a sync could lose a name, an avatar, a set of handles or a name color. They merge without losing anything.
- A wanted game put its system in a filter that could never show it.
- A memory with no day was filed on 1 January.
- A day holding both a session and a memory appeared as two separate sections.
- Journal day cells were readable by VoiceOver but never announced as controls.
- Calendar day numbers vanished into bright cover art.
- The "…" on a game page was invisible over bright key art.
- The precision picker had grown a sixth option that truncated before you touched it.
- American spellings throughout, including two that had shipped.
