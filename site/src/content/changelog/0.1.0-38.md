Home used to decide for you what came first. It was a fair guess — what you're playing, then what's next — but it's your page, and the guess said nothing about who you are.

### Home is yours to arrange [new]

**The display case.** Your consoles sit under your name in a three-wide case, six at a time, the rest behind See all. Press and hold it for **Arrange Systems**: how many reach Home, the order — drag it, or pick North American release order, most games, or A–Z — and whether you'd rather have a scrolling row than a case. Someone with four systems will want the row; it's a switch.

**Every shelf is a choice.** Press and hold any shelf for **Arrange Home**: drag the blocks, switch any of them off, and bring Backlog or Finished up from Library if you want them here. This order follows you to your other devices — a self-portrait isn't a per-device setting.

**See all shows the consoles.** The case holds six; "See all" opens every console you own as a grid, in your order. Tap one for your games on it.

**A collection can be a shelf.** "Six That Made Me" on Home, its covers under its name, says more than a tile in a row of tiles. Open a collection and choose **Show on Home** from its ⋯ menu, or press and hold its card in Library. Remove it the same way.

**A collection can fill itself.** Set Library's filters — Backlog, on the SNES, physical — and **save those filters as a collection**. It's a Smart Collection: add a game that fits and it's in, change the game and it may leave. It can live on Home like any other.

**A new library leads with the question.** Before a single game, Home asks which consoles are yours.

### Maps, with pins that mean something [new]

A game page has a **Maps** section now. A map is a picture you own of the game — from your Photo Library, from Files, pasted, or found: give the finder a wiki page and it reads the map images off it, checks each one exists, and keeps the site it came from with the map.

Open one and pinch, pan, and drop pins — press and hold anywhere, or use Place to pan the map under a ring. A pin is a collectible, a note, a warning, or a secret; the legend at the bottom is also the filter. Mark a pin **explored** and it dims with a check; **Left** and **Explored** split what's still out there from what isn't.

**Link a pin to a tracker item and they become one thing.** The pin takes the item's name, shows its checkbox, and ticking either one ticks the other — with the same journal line. From the tracker, an item with a pin says "On the map"; one without offers **Pin on Map…** and opens the map with the item already chosen, two taps from "where is it" to a pin that ticks.

Maps sync with your library, go to Recently Deleted when you delete them, and come with an export.

### Seven colors, and nothing to get wrong [new]

The color picker is seven circles. Pick one for the **accent** and it's the same color on both grounds — writing in its darker step on light, as itself on dark, so nothing ever needs correcting. Pick one for the **background** and the ground becomes that color laid over the app's grey and charcoal, which cannot make text unreadable however you choose. The preview shows both grounds at once, side by side. The hue plane, the hex field and the saved swatches are gone; so is "Match light and dark", because the palette is the matching.

Buttons look the way the palette does: the accent as the fill, its step as the ink and as a hard edge beneath. Play on Home, Start Session and Place on a map lead; no more white text on a light ground.

### Your consoles are things you own [new]

A console used to be a word on a game. It's a record now: **how** you have it — the same nine ways a game can be yours — **when you got it**, and a photograph of the actual machine on your shelf.

**It fills itself in.** Add a game on the SNES and the SNES is yours; a physical game on a console you only emulate asks first, once, and remembers your answer. Nothing is ever assumed from a display copy.

**Which one you had.** A platform is thirty years of different objects, so the console page asks which machine to draw: the Funtastic N64 or the charcoal one, the front-loading NES or the top-loader, a Switch Lite in coral, a Game Boy in Play It Loud yellow. Underneath, **Machines you have had** keeps the whole lineage, oldest first — the Mac from 1984 and the one on your desk today.

Consoles export with your library, and deleting one leaves it in Recently Deleted for thirty days.

### A color for light and another for dark [new]

The palette is seven pairs plus Mono, and each appearance gets its own. Tap the light half of the preview and pick; tap the dark half and pick something else. Follow the system and the app changes with it — accent, ground, widgets and all. "Use the default colors" puts both back to what an empty library looks like.

The play controls answer your finger now: a firm tap when you start or resume, a lighter one for pause and stop, and a small success when a session is logged.

### The console set, redrawn [new]

Ninety-odd machines, drawn again with nothing plugged in: ports empty, the controller sitting in front. Every logo is back where the real hardware has it, and the shells you can choose from now run to the Funtastic N64s, the Play It Loud Game Boys, the Game Boy Color's berry and kiwi and teal, the GBA and its SP, the DS Lite, the 3DS, the GameCube, the Switch Lite and the OLED.

### Choose many games at once [new]

**Library or any console page → Filter → Select Games…**. Tap the ones you mean — each gets a check and an accent outline — then set the console you own them on, or their status, for all of them together. It's the fastest way to fix a hundred games that came in from another app on the wrong system.

### Bringing a library in [new]

**Settings → Your data → Import from CSV** reads an export from Gamery, Backloggd, a spreadsheet, or anywhere else. A row that carries an **IGDB id** is matched by it, exactly, rather than guessed at from its title — so an export that has them arrives with nothing to check. A cell naming two systems ("Xbox,Mac") makes the game yours on both. Everything is reviewed before a single game is added, and it waits if iCloud is still bringing your library down.

### Also new

- **Your series, from Wikidata.** When IGDB filed a game under no franchise, Game Info's Series line and the "More from…" shelf use Wikidata's series instead.
- **Paste a Google Sheets link.** In a tracker's list importer, paste the sheet's link and Read: the tab's rows come in for review like any pasted list. No account, no key. If the sheet's owner has turned off downloading, it says so.
- **The case is six across** on iPad and Mac, one row of hardware instead of a phone grid stretched.
- **Continue Playing is three things, not one.** The cover and the words open the game; Play, Stop, and Resume are their own buttons beside it.
- **Settings in dark mode** has a solid ground behind every page, so covers no longer bleed through the sheet — and every page stands on the ground you chose.
- **Move a pin.** Open it and choose Move Pin; the map pans under the ring and Place becomes "Move here".
- **Delete a memory**, from its sheet or its row's press-and-hold. It waits in Recently Deleted for 30 days with its pictures.
- **Maps sit after the tracker** on the game page, and Compact is the default tracker layout.
- **Ownership & access, Custom order:** the chips grow a grip and drag onto each other, right in the row.
- **The systems case** folds a console stored under two spellings into one tile, and its press-and-hold offers Arrange Home and Hide from Home like every other shelf.
- **A busy spreadsheet tab** — a banner, a list, and calculators beside it — reads as the list, named by its own header.
- **Recently Deleted** names a map. **Search** shows only the collections a match is in. On **iPad** your name and picture are centered and a size up. The tracker page wears the game's logo as its title.
- **Memories in Recently Deleted.** The one thing you could write that couldn't be recovered. Restoring a memory brings back the pictures that left with it.
- The in-app question waits until a game has been in your library for a day, rather than appearing beside the first title you type.
- **Ownership & access** — the settings row's new name, since five of its nine words aren't ownership.

- **Swipe through the pictures.** Open a screenshot and swipe for the next one; tap another trailer and it replaces the one playing, without closing anything first.
- **Spin the art in place.** Drag a game's cover or logo on its page and it tilts and springs back. Right-click either one — press and hold on a phone — for all three artwork choices.
- **Three columns on a wide window.** Open the tracker and the videos on a Mac window wider than 1,400pt and the game page stays beside them rather than sliding off.
- **All Game Pages is a window of its own on the Mac**, so you can drag it off the header you are changing and watch the page change behind it.
- **The Mac saves where you say.** Export opens a real save panel, every settings page wears the app's colors, and Escape closes a sheet.

### Fixed

- A memory written after 8 PM Eastern was filed on tomorrow. It's filed on the day you wrote it, in every time zone.
- Adding a map from the Photo Library opened nothing. It opens the picker.
- The ⋯ button's shadow in light mode read as a grey smear. It's a quarter of the weight there.
- The Theme & colors page kept the old accent on its own icon after you changed it, and its swatch drew the ink rather than the color you picked.
- A game page layout that no longer exists ("banner", from an old build) repairs itself to Showcase instead of syncing forever.
- Scrolling down a game page and then resizing the window sent you back to the top. It keeps your place, both directions.
- A ground chosen for dark mode was being worn by light mode's Home Screen widget, and a game page kept the app's built-in purple instead of the color you chose.
- The color editor could show colors you were not wearing after iCloud synced a change; the app now re-reads the theme whenever a sync lands.
- On the Mac, the settings sheet's Done sat on every page, above the color editor's own Cancel and Done. It belongs to the settings home page.
