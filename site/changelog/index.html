0.1.0 (22) — 17 August 2026

Regenerating a tracker can't quietly cost you progress any more.

New

  • Choose what a regeneration does. The button now says what it will do — add only what's new, review the changes first, or replace everything — and you can pick a different one for a single run without changing your default. Add-only is the default, because it's the one option that can't cost you anything.
  • Review changes before they happen. See what a generation would add, remove and rename, grouped by category, and tick exactly what you want. Nothing is written until you say so.
  • Paste a list. Drop in a community checklist — a markdown table, or sections of numbered items — and it becomes real tracker categories. No AI, no waiting, and nothing invented: it reads what you paste, exactly as written. Imported lists are locked, so a later regeneration can't replace them.
  • Your own notes on any item. Write whatever you want to remember. Your note is yours — regenerating the tracker never touches it.
  • Rename anything. Categories and items, including generated ones. A generator's naming is a suggestion; if the game calls them Achievements, call them Achievements.

Improved

  • Progress follows renamed items. Generation doesn't name things the same way twice. Previously that could silently reset your checkmarks; now progress moves with the item, and anything genuinely dropped can be kept as a personal goal instead.
  • Long checklists are far shorter. Items sharing a location group under it once instead of repeating it on every row, and short items lay out in columns — one on a phone, more when there's room.
  • Checkboxes are easier to hit. Tap targets were smaller than they should have been.
  • Timer controls are available with the tracker and a guide open side by side on iPad and Mac.

Fixed

  • Tapping a second video in the list now actually changes the player — and each video keeps its own resume position instead of inheriting another's.
  • Every part of a playlist remembers where you got to, rather than the playlist remembering only one position across all of them.
  • Run logging works on AI-generated trackers. "Log Runs for This Game" was a no-op on exactly the games most likely to want it.
  • The Runs section no longer appears twice on iPad and Mac in compact tracker display.
  • Generation keeps running when you move around the app, with elapsed time and a clearer sense of how long it takes.

0.1.0 (21) — 16 August 2026

First build available to beta testers outside my own devices.

  • Use the built-in tracker. Some games ship with a hand-built tracker. If you'd generated over one, you can now switch back.
  • Console art for PS3, Android, iPhone and iPad on the systems shelf.
  • An honest note about how this app is made.

0.1.0 — the beta so far

Rather than reconstruct every build, here's what LevelSelect already was when the beta opened:

  • A private library. Your games, sessions and progress, synced through your own iCloud. No account, no ads, no analytics, no tracking.
  • Session tracking with Live Activities, Home Screen widgets and an Apple Watch app.
  • Objective trackers — checklists, collectibles and ranked upgrades — generated for a game or hand-built, with multiple playthroughs tracked separately.
  • Run logging for roguelikes, kept separate from play time.
  • Guides and videos attached to a game, with playback position remembered.
  • Systems shelves with original console artwork.
  • Import and export. Bring a library in from a CSV, and take everything out again as readable JSON whenever you like.
